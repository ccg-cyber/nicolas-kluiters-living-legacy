Biography · 1940–1985

From Delft to the Bekaa

A Dutch artist entered the Society of Jesus, trained in Lebanon, and spent his priestly and social ministry among villages in the northern Bekaa.

Art, formation and vocation

Nicolas Kluiters was born in Delft, the Netherlands, on 25 May 1940. He studied fine arts before joining the Jesuits; Jesuit accounts describe a man who remained an artist after choosing priesthood. This archive does not reproduce quotations attributed to him unless a published, traceable source can be identified.

A Jesuit biography dates his entry into the Society of Jesus to 1966, the year he arrived in Lebanon for formation. He studied Arabic and social work in Beirut and completed priestly formation. He was ordained in Amsterdam in the summer of 1973. These milestones appear in Jesuit and press accounts, including biographical material.

Lebanon: language, society and ministry

Saint Joseph University reports that Kluiters learned Arabic and studied social work in Lebanon. This background helps explain why his work was not only pastoral: he sought to understand the daily conditions of villages marginalized by poor infrastructure and war.

By the mid-1970s he was serving villages in the Bekaa. Sources differ on the exact year he moved to Barqa: reports cite 1974, 1976 and 1977, while another refers to a later appointment. We use “mid-1970s” in the timeline and explain the discrepancy instead of fixing a date without adequate evidence.

An artist of reconciliation

Jesuit accounts and interviews with people who knew him describe efforts to bring people together through social and pastoral work, and to encourage reconciliation among families and neighboring communities. A Jesuit retrospective says a live Stations of the Cross became an annual Barqa tradition around 1980. These details are reported memories, not an independent administrative record for every project.

The sources closest to his life portray him as priest, artist and social worker at once—not as an abstract hero detached from the people who worked alongside him.

Editorial note from the archive

Kidnapping and death

On 14 March 1985, Father Nicolas was kidnapped in the Bekaa during the Lebanese Civil War. The Jesuit Conference of European Provincials reports that his body was found in a deep ravine on 1 April. We do not name a perpetrator or motive here: the sources reviewed do not establish a single, independently documented account.

A Lebanese press report identifies his tomb at Deir Taanayel. We describe this as a published report with a photograph, not as an ecclesiastical register of relics or an approved pilgrimage site.
